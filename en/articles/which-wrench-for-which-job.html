Which wrench for which job? A guide to the six common types

By M.T.T. Hardware, Sampheng · Published 4 Oct 2026

Hardware shops sell many kinds of wrench, and each suits different work. Pick the wrong one and the job is awkward, and you can round off the nut. This guide covers the wrench types in the WYNNTOOLS catalogue, which one fits which job, and where to start.

Six common wrench types

1

Adjustable wrench: one tool, many sizes

Turn the worm to set the jaw width, so one wrench fits many nut sizes. It suits a toolbag or general jobs around the house. The jaw always has a little play, so on a very tight nut it can slip and round the corners. Close the jaw snugly on the nut every time before you pull.

2

Combination wrench: the everyday workhorse

One end is open, the other a ring of the same size. Use the open end to run a nut on quickly, then the ring to break it loose or tighten it, because the ring grips the whole nut. The WYNNTOOLS combination wrenches are CR-V steel with the ring end offset at 15°.

3

Double ring wrench: for real force

A ring at each end in two sizes, such as 8x10 mm. A ring holds all six corners, so it slips far less than an open end, which makes it the one for seized nuts or worn heads. The catalogue's double ring wrenches have both rings offset at 75° for easier use.

4

Ratchet wrench: keep turning without lifting off

The ring end has a ratchet, so you keep turning without taking the wrench off the nut. It saves a lot of time on long threads or in tight spots. The catalogue has 72-tooth ratchet rings and double-ended 12-point flex-head ratchet wrenches.

5

Sockets and handles: the kit for cars and workshops

Swap the socket to match the nut and drive it with a ratchet handle. The socket's drive size must match the handle, for example 1/2 inch or 3/8 inch. The catalogue's 1/2-inch ratchet handles have a 24-tooth gear, a compact head and left-right switching.

6

Hex and Torx keys: for socket-head screws

For screws with a hexagon or star-shaped socket in the head, common in flat-pack furniture, bicycles and machinery. Ball-end keys can enter the screw at an angle; one ball-end set in the catalogue works at up to 25°, which helps where you can't get in straight.

Building a first kit

If you own no wrenches yet, start with three things: a mid-size adjustable wrench, a set of combination wrenches and a hex key set. That covers most jobs around the house. Adjustable wrenches are sized by length, for example 8 inches or 200 mm.

The catalogue has combination wrench sets from 6 to 14 pieces, plus phosphated adjustable wrenches under our own M.T.T. brand. If you work on vehicles too, add sockets and a ratchet handle later.

Common questions

What's the difference between an adjustable wrench and an open-end wrench?

An adjustable wrench changes size, so one tool fits many nuts, but the jaw has some play. An open-end wrench is a fixed size, so you need a set, but it holds the nut more firmly.

Ring or open end: which is better?

A ring holds the whole nut and rarely slips, so use it when you need force. An open end goes on and off faster and fits where a ring can't be slipped over from above. That's why a combination wrench has one of each.

What does CR-V mean?

Chromium-vanadium steel. It's widely used for wrenches and sockets because it is strong and resists wear.

What size is a 4-hun socket?

Four hun is a 1/2-inch square drive, used with a 1/2-inch handle; three hun is 3/8 inch. The socket and the handle must have the same drive size.
