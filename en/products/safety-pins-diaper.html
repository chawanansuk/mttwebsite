Why cloth diapers still need pins

Disposables are more convenient, but cloth diapers remain the choice in many homes: washable, far cheaper over time, and gentler on skin that reacts to synthetics. Cloth needs a fastener, and the size-4 pin has long been the standard — long enough for folded cloth, with a cap over the point. Hospitals and nurseries still use them.

Safe diaper-pinning technique
  1. Slide your hand between fabric and baby while pinning.
  2. Pin horizontally, cap outward, point away from the tummy.
  3. Check the cap clicks shut; replace any pin with a tired spring.

FAQ

Which size for diapers?
Size 4 (5.6 cm), a.k.a. the blanket pin — long enough for folded cloth and easy to handle. See the size-4 page for full specs.
Is it safe for babies?
The capped point and firm spring keep it secure — follow the technique above, and ask us about plastic-head locking pins for extra safety.
Can the pins be washed?
Always remove pins before washing the cloth. Rinse them in mild soapy water and dry completely before storing — moisture is the enemy, since these are nickel-plated steel rather than true stainless. Kept dry, they last for years.
Can size 3 replace size 4?
Yes for thinner cloth. Size 3 is 46 mm against size 4's 56 mm — fine for light muslin folded a few times, but thick cotton or many folds pin much more easily with size 4, and the pin is less likely to bend.
Is a loose spring still usable?
Not for baby work. If the cap feels loose or springs open easily, take it out of service — pins cost very little next to the risk. Test by closing it and tugging gently; if the cap lifts on its own, the spring is done.
Can shops buy to resell?
Yes — a 432-pin box at wholesale splits into 12-pin bunches with a strong retail margin.
